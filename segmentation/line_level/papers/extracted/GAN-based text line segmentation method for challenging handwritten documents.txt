Abstract
Text line segmentation (TLS) is an essential step of the end-to-end document analysis systems. The main purpose of this
step is to extract the individual text lines of any handwritten documents with high accuracy. Handwritten and historical
documents mostly contain touching and overlapping characters, heavy diacritics, footnotes and side notes added over the
years. In this work, we present a new TLS method based on generative adversarial networks (GAN). TLS problem is tackled
as an image-to-image translation problem and the GAN model was trained to learn the spatial information between the
individual text lines and their corresponding masks including the text lines. To evaluate the segmentation performance of
the proposed GAN model, two challenging datasets, VML-AHTE and VML-MOC, were used. According to the qualitative
and quantitative results, the proposed GAN model achieved the best segmentation accuracy on the VML-MOC dataset and
showed competitive performance on the VML-AHTE dataset.

2 Related works
2.1 Learning-free methods for TLS
Various learning-free methods have been proposed for the
TLS problem so far. Projection-based methods [1, 2, 32],
Hough transform-based methods [20–22], and connected
component-based methods [16, 30] are examples of learning-
free methods frequently used in the literature. In projection-
based methods, it’s assumed that the text line is straight
and segmentation is done by using the spaces between adja-
cent text lines. While projection-based methods are often
successful in printed documents [2], they fail especially in
manuscript documents containing skewed and curved text
lines. Successful text line segmentation can be done up to
a certain level by using piece-wise projections in handwrit-
ten documents that do not have multi-skewed text lines and
do not contain too much complexity [1]. However, the range
of document images that projection-based methods can suc-
cessfully segment text lines is limited. Moreover,projection-
based methods cannot perform successful segmentation due
to the situations that make segmentation in handwritten doc-
uments difﬁcult. Hough transform-based methods assume
that the text line components are ﬂat or with a low angle of
inclination. For this reason, Hough transform-based meth-
ods can perform successful text line segmentation in printed
document images, but not for handwritten document images.
In Hough transform-based methods, especially in languages
such as Arabic, where small components used for punctua-
tionandemphasisaredense,thesegmentationperformanceis
considerably reduced in case of curvature between text lines.
In connected component-based methods, each pixel is usu-
ally of equal importance. Therefore, connected component-
based methods fail especially if the manuscript has touching
and overlapping text lines. The above-mentioned learning-
free methods cannot correctly segment text line documents
with vertical, multi-skewed, and multi-directed text lines,
documents with curved and ﬂuctuated text lines, and doc-
uments with touching and overlapping characters or words.
Until the last decade, the text line segmentation problem was
usually tried to be solved with learning-free methods. How-
ever, to get rid of the constraints of learning-free methods,
with the increase in computer processing capacities and data
amount, machine learning and CNN-based text line segmen-
tation methods have been used more frequently recently.
2.2 CNN-based methods for TLS
As learning-based approaches, handwritten text line seg-
mentation methods using a fully convolutional network are
proposed [3, 4, 24, 28, 33]. In [27], baseline detection on his-
torical documents is performed using a convolutional neural
network-based method. In another learning-based approach,
text lines are segmented as X-height using adaptive U-net
[23]. Fully convolutional neural network variants U-net [29]
and RU-net are used for Arabic handwritten document seg-
mentation [26]. In [12], text lines are extracted as baselines
with ARU-Net based on U-net architecture. In [8], Mask R-
CNN is used as a holistic method for text-line extraction
in handwritten documents. The segmentation performance
of the proposed method is evaluated by implementing vari-
ous datasets including different kinds of challenges such as
touching, overlapping characters, and diacritics. In [13], the

2.3 GANs for TLS and document analysis
Generative adversarial networks (GAN) [6], which is one of
the deep learning methods used as an effective method to
generate images recently, have also been used to segment
text lines in document images [7, 17]. In [17], the text line
segmentation performance of the proposed GAN model is
evaluated for different combinations of the GAN loss, L1
loss, and L2 loss functions. The GAN model is applied on
two different datasets consisting of manuscript document
images and the best text line segmentation performance is
achieved with the GAN model in which U-Net [29] is used
as a generator and PatchGAN [14] is used as a discriminator.
In our previous work, text line segmentation is performed on
a dataset consisting of Arabic manuscripts with compelling
features in terms of segmentation with GAN [7]. Another
important application of GAN is the segmentation of texts
from touching text and non-text components [25]. For this
purpose, a GAN model with ResNet as a generator and Patch-
Gan as a discriminator is proposed. The results show that the
proposed GAN model can efﬁciently predict the text regions
from the combined (text and non-text) images.
3 Method
As already mentioned, handwritten documents have several
challenging problems for accurate text line segmentation
(TLS).Amongthesechallenges,thepresenceoftouchingand
overlapping characters, and skewed and multi-directed lines
are the most bottleneck problems. In this work, we focused
on producing a model that can overcome these problems in
text line segmentation. For this purpose, the TLS problem
was considered as an image-to-image translation problem
and the proposed model was trained to generate the optimal
mask for each text line. Here, the mask corresponds to a pla-
nar structure covering all characters and punctuation marks
in an individual text line. The main purpose of the image-
to-image translation is to transfer images from one domain
to another by performing operations such as color space,
histogram transformations, style, or characteristic transfer.
Hereby, in this work, image-to-image translation was uti-
lized to map the spatial information from the original image
domain to the mask image domain and so generate a mask
for each text line. A generative adversarial network (GAN) is
one of the powerful CNN-based models for image-to-image
translation tasks. In this work, we’ve been inspired by the
pix2pix model [14]. pix2pix model is composed of two com-
petitive CNN models, U-Net and PatchGAN. U-Net is used
in the generator part of the model, and PatchGAN is used
in the discriminator part. These models play a min–max
game, whereby the generator aims to produce better syn-
thetic images and the discriminator updates itself to better
discriminate the synthetic images produced by the generator.
Text lines in document images with challenging segmen-
tation characteristics were segmented using a generative
adversarial network. The block diagram of the proposed
method is given in Fig.1. In the training phase of the net-
work, original document images and ground truth images
containing text line masks were used. The GAN network
model was trained to learn the spatial relationship between
the original manuscript texts and the mask images of the text
lines. Training and test data were created based on a data set
consisting of Arabic texts with challenging characteristics.
Moreover, in order to examine the effect of loss functions on
the text line segmentation, the training process of the network
was carried out using three different loss functions, L1, L2,
and GAN loss function.
3.1 Dataset
SinceGANandCNN-basedmodelsarehighlydata-dependent,
the content and diversity of the dataset for accurate model
training are crucial. From this viewpoint, a dataset consist-
ing of two datasets with different text line characteristics was
generated. The whole dataset has many samples involving
challenges in text line segmentation, which is the motivation
for this work. Training and test data were generated from the
whole dataset. Apart from the test data, to evaluate the over-
all segmentation performance of the proposed GAN model,
a separate test dataset was utilized. The characteristics of the
datasets used and the processing of the data for the training
and the evaluation of the GAN model are explained below.
3.1.1 VML-AHTE dataset: Arabic handwritten text line
extraction dataset
VML-AHTE dataset [5] has characters that touch and overlap
between adjacent text lines and too many diacritics. Out of a
total of 30 pp. of high-resolution images in the dataset, 20 of
them are set for training and 10 for testing. An example of a
document image in the dataset and its corresponding ground
truth image are given in Fig.2.

text line dataset
The text lines in this dataset containing Arabic manuscripts,
containing side notes over the years, are highly skewed and
curved. In the VML-MOC dataset [18], there are 20 images
for training and 10 for testing. In this dataset, text lines in
ground truth images are represented by masks covering the

3.2 Evaluation
The pix2pix-based GAN Model designed for text line seg-
mentation is given in Fig.5. In the proposed method, the
U-Net network model [29], which is also used in the seg-
mentation of medical images as a generative network, is
employed. The U-Net architecture consists of two parts:
the encoder and the decoder. In the encoder part, the input
image is transformed into feature maps using convolution
and max-pooling layers. Each convolutional layer learns spe-
ciﬁc features of the image and produces a lower-resolution
feature map, providing information about the general struc-
ture of the image. The transpose convolution layers in the
decoder part rescale the feature maps, effectively increasing
the size and bringing back the details in the image to gen-
erate the target image. Each downsampling block contains a
3 × 3 convolution, batch normalization, and a leaky ReLU
layer with a slope of 0.2. In the upsampling blocks, there
is a transposed convolution, Batch Normalization, a Dropout
layer with a 50% rate, and a leaky ReLU layer with a slope of
0.2. Moreover, it includes skip connections, which associate
the feature maps from the encoder part with higher-resolution
output, allowing the network to learn better features and pro-
duce better results. In this work, the generative network takes
original document images as input and produces text line
masks as output. The number of convolution or transpose
convolution layers used in the encoder and decoder parts

4 Results and discussion
In our work, the text line is expressed with a mask to cover
all the components in the text lines. We’ve trained three dif-
ferent GAN models concerning three loss deﬁnitions. These
GAN models were implemented to different test datasets,
obtained from VML-MOC, and VML-AHTE datasets. So,
we’ve analyzed the segmentation performance of the models
for different losses. GAN models can create noisy compo-
nents in generated mask images in each training scenario. To
clean up these noisy components, components smaller than
a certain pixel size in the network outputs are deleted by the
connected component analysis. Qualitative and quantitative
results for each dataset are given in the following subsections.
4.1 Test results on VML-AHTE
As stated before, the VML-AHTE dataset contains chal-
lenges such as straight text lines with diacritics, and touching
and overlapping characters between adjacent text lines. So,
using this dataset makes it possible to determine the segmen-
tation performance of the proposed GAN model under these
challenges. Figure7 implies that the GAN model mostly
generates masks in accordance with the diacritics and touch-
ing/overlapping characters. As a comparison, segmentation
results of the GAN model are given and compared with the
ones of state-of-the-art unsupervised and supervised learn-
ing methods in Table 1. According to the numerical results,
GAN models show very competitive results with other state-
of-the-art methods. For the DR, RA, and F M metrics, the
GAN model with L1 loss has the best performance of pro-
posed GAN models and second best performance among all
methods. For the IoU metrics, the GAN model with original
GAN Loss has the second-best performance for Line IU and
third-best for the Pixel IU.

As shown in Fig.8 successful text line segmentation has
been achieved even if the text lines are in different directions
in regions where the text lines are more regular and have

line or components such as notes and ornaments added to
the end of the line can reduce the segmentation accuracy. As

The numerical results show that the proposed GAN model
performs good segmentation performance on two different
challenging datasets, VML-AHTE and VML-MOC. On the
VML-AHTE dataset, including narrow text lines with heavy
diacritics, the GAN models achieved a good overall seg-
mentation accuracy and failed to generate separate masks
where there are nested text lines with touching and overlap-
ping characters. The proposed GAN model achieved the best
segmentation results among all supervised and unsupervised
methods on the VML-MOC dataset. It implies that the GAN
model can learn and generate masks for heavily skewed and
curved text lines. However, in the case of sudden changes
in the orientation of neighboring text lines, the GAN model
predicts a single text line behavior and generates a mask for
these text lines.
5 Conclusion
In this work, a GAN-based text line segmentation method has
been proposed. TLS problem was considered an image-to-
image translation problem and the GAN model was trained to
learn the spatial information of text lines within patch images
by generating text line masks. The GAN model was trained
with images from two challenging datasets, VML-AHTE and
VML-MOC.Themainreasonforselectingthesedatasetswas
to generate a model that can segment the skewed/curved text
lines that exhibit rich diacritics, and touching/overlapping
characters. In our work, we’ve trained the GAN model with
different loss functions; GAN Loss, L1, and L2 loss. The
resulting GAN models were tested on test images of VML-
AHTE and VML-MOC datasets.
The results show that the proposed GAN model is very
effective for the segmentation of the text lines of challenging
handwritten documents and has a very high potential to be
developed for this crucial problem in handwritten and his-
torical document analysis. Considering that most historical
documents contain skewed and curved text lines, touch-
ing and overlapping characters, footnotes, and/or side notes
added with different orientations to the original manuscripts
over the years, GAN models seem to have the ability to over-
come these expectations.
Even though the text line segmentation problem has been
resolved in printed documents, it remains a challenging and
unresolved issue for handwritten documents.TLS techniques
represent one of the fundamental steps employed in docu-
ment analysis systems and are typically utilized in the initial
stages of these systems. Consequently, errors made at this
stage tend to propagate, leading to more signiﬁcant errors
throughout the system. Therefore, resolving the text line
segmentation problem with high accuracy will enhance the
accuracy of document analysis systems.TLS techniques are
utilized in various tasks such as optical character recog-
nition, handwritten document analysis, transcription tasks,
document image understanding, word spotting, writer iden-
tiﬁcation, content-based image retrieval, analysis of medical
records, historical population records, and court records,
among many other processes involving the analysis of hand-
written documents. Although the main motivation of this
work is on text line segmentation in challenging handwritten
Arabic documents, the proposed model can also be modi-
ﬁed and used in document de-warping tasks [10] following
additional pre-processing steps
The proposed method has achieved more successful
results in the text line segmentation task on the VML-
AHTE dataset compared to the images in the VML-MOC
dataset. The text lines in the images in the VML-AHTE
dataset exhibit a more regular arrangement, while those in the
VML-MOC dataset are skewed, curved, multi-directional,
and irregular. The proposed method may make incorrect seg-
mentation in cases where there is excessive space between
two words in a text line or sudden changes in the direction of
the line. By analyzing the gaps between lines of text, regions
with more gaps than normal can be identiﬁed and it is thought
that segmentation success can be increased by applying a
different segmentation technique or post-processing steps in
these areas. Similarly, various ﬁlters can be used to detect the
orientation of lines of text, thus improving the accuracy of
line segmentation by applying a more precise segmentation
technique in areas where line orientations change rapidly.
Future work will include text line segmentation on
Ottoman archival documents, which pose similar challenges
to the datasets used in this paper and are studied by histori-
ans and researchers from around the world. For this purpose,
we aim to create a large dataset, overcome the shortcomings
of our method with various post-processing techniques, and
segment text lines in Ottoman archival documents. More-
over, we plan to develop custom GAN models and present
a holistic method for text line segmentation in challenging
historical documents.
