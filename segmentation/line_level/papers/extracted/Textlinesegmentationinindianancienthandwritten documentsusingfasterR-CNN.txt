Text line segmentation in indian ancient handwritten
documents using faster R-CNN
Amar Jindal1 · Rajib Ghosh1
Abstract
Textline segmentation in ancient handwritten documents is still considered as a challeng-
ing task in document analysis and recognition field even though various rule-based methods
exist. These methods succeed under constraint such as a roughly uniform background. They
do not contribute well in case of variable inter-line spacing and overlapping characters. This
article proposes faster region-convolution neural network (R-CNN) based robust method to
segment the textlines in the ancient handwritten document in Devanagari script for the first
time in literature. The feature matrix has been generated by residual network and proposals
have been predicted through the region proposal network (RPN). A pooling layer has been
used to extract regions of interest, known as region of interest pooling layer, to locate the
textlines. The performance of the proposed textline segmentation system has been evalu-
ated on self generated dataset of ancient handwritten documents in Devanagari script and it
has achieved the f-measure of 99.98%. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed
system outperforms the existing state-of-the-art methods of textline segmentation.
Keywords Text line segmentation · Ancient documents · Devanagari script · Faster R-CNN
1 Introduction
Any handwritten text recognition task can be performed in two different modes—online
and offline. In the online mode of recognition, the text is written with the help of a pen like
stylus on a tablet and the handwritten text is instantly recognized by the system at the very
moment of writing it on the tab. In this recognition mode, movement of pen-tip and time
series of coordinates are collected during text collection [5, 11]. In offline mode, the text is
written on a piece of paper and the image of the document is fed to the computer through
optical scanning. The text present in the scanned image is recognized by the system later [4,
21, 22]. In the recent decades, intensive works are going on offline handwritten text recog-
nition (HTR) in ancient historical documents [7, 14, 18]. In the southern part of the Asia

A textline can be defined as ruling to the text which shows the position and presence of
the text within a page. It is also used for layout analysis of the document and basis enhance-
ment of the readability. Textline segmentation (TLS) is the very first step to start building
any OCR for multi-sentenced documents. So, several investigations have been reported [3,
10, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 22, 24, 29] to segment the textlines in ancient handwritten docu-
ments, but most of these investigations have been carried out in Latin, Chinese, Arabic,
and Japanese scripts. These existing studies have mostly relied upon various conventional
techniques like projection profile [21, 22], component grouping [13, 15, 24], and hough
transform [17] based methods to segment the textlines in various ancient documents. But
these conventional techniques are not capable to overcome all the challenges like stains of
ink and humidity on text, faded text, and inter-connection of two consecutive lines present
in the ancient historical documents because these techniques follow a fixed mathematical
approach and require prior knowledge of the layout structure of the documents. The con-
ventional techniques consume more time to extract features at all the points of the document
image. Few of the conventional techniques such as projection profile based methods fail
to segment the textlines correctly if the interline overlapping and skewness of the text are
present in the document because these methods cannot find any row containing white pixels

This article proposes a faster region-convolutional neural network (R-CNN) [26] model
based method for TLS in ancient historical handwritten Devanagari document images for
the first time in the literature. The present work has used the residual network Resnet50 as
a backbone architecture in faster R-CNN for the first time in this research domain (TLS
in ancient handwritten Devanagari document images). Faster R-CNN is a deep learning
architecture used for object detection using a region proposal algorithm. Faster R-CNN
provides very high object detection accuracy in comparison to other convolution neural
network (CNN) based architectures due to the generation of various region proposals using
the region proposal algorithm. It drastically reduces the training time and testing time for
the detection network. By exploiting the capability of the faster R-CNN detection network,
the textlines of the ancient handwritten historical documents can be detected easily because
textline can also be seen as a bounding box [20] or a set of connected text pixels [31].
Moreover, if the problems like faded text, interline overlapping, and skewness of the text are
present in the documents, then the existing methods fail to solve these problems, whereas the
proposed faster R-CNN based method resolves these problems in most of the samples except
few as it relies on the generation of various region proposals using the region proposal
algorithm.

2 Related work
As already mentioned a little number of studies are available for handwritten text recog-
nition in the ancient historical document in Devanagari script. Most of the studies have
focused on various non-Indian scripts. These existing studies in both Indian and non-Indian
scripts are discussed below briefly under two different categories, Ad hoc and machine
learning based methods.
2.1 Ad hoc methods
Narang et al. [21] have developed a complete ancient Devanagari handwritten text recog-
nition system on self generated data. For textline segmentation, the piece-wise projection
profile method has been used where an image has been divided into fixed strips and then
the horizontal projection profile has been applied in each strip. The problem of under-
segmentation and over-segmentation has been resolved by computing the average height
of the textline for each piece-wise strip and then applying the projection profile method in
another study [22] on ancient historical handwritten document in Devanagari script. The
projection profile based methods are not able to provide a very high TLS accuracy when
a large amount of noise is present, variable inter-line spacing exists, and touching charac-
ters are present in almost every textline of the document image. Similarly, the seam curving
between the two adjacent lines has been used to segment the textlines in ancient historical
documents. To find the medial seam, Saabni et al. [29] have divided the image into a fixed
number of strips and then the Sobel operator has been applied to observe the horizontal
seam.
The grouping methodology involves the alignment of text units by clustering them in a
bottom-up approach. Black pixels of primitive components i.e. text characters are grouped
to form a unit. These connected components are further joined to find out textline align-
ment. The challenges of this bottom-up grouping strategy like identifying factual alignment
of textline, overlapping textline, and choosing nearest neighbour have been resolved by iter-
ative perceptual grouping in [15]. In another study [13], the average height and width of
each character in the textline are considered to be equal. The initials of the textline are iden-
tified by the average height threshold of the connected component. But, the major drawback
of this method is it fails if the average height and width of the character are not equal. Garz
10706
Multimedia Tools and Applications (2023) 82:10703–10722
et al. [3] have used the grouping methodology without performing binarization on the doc-
ument image samples from the Saint-Gall dataset. The clustering interest points have been
extracted using gaussian difference at locations of local minima and local maxima which
represent different portions of the character.
Rabaev et al. [24] have extracted the potential characters to identify a textline using con-
nected components. The sweep line algorithm has been applied to find out the position of
each potential character element to identify a textline. In another study [10], on one of the
Indic scripts, both text and non-text portions were present in the same ancient historical
documents. The study applied the grouping clustering technique to perform the segmenta-
tion of text and non-text. Two clusters were formed because the space difference between
the text and non-text portions is larger as compared to each of them. However, the grouping
based methodology is not able to calculate the correct component size due to the pixels of
faded character.
Messaoud et al. [19] proposed a combination of three different methods as a multilevel
TLS framework. At the first level, the low threshold projection profile method was applied
for samples not containing the interline overlapping of textlines and horizontal textlines.
For the small amount of touching components, the grouping methodology was used as a
second level TLS. In the third level, the huge overlapping between the interline words was
resolved by calculating the nearest neighbour of the words. Louloudis et al. [17] have ini-
tially calculated the connected components and average height of the character. The subsets
of connected components were created based on the fixed threshold of the average height
and average width of the character and components. The Hough transform mapping was
applied to each subset. In the final step of the post-processing, the subsets were merged
into identified textlines. To identify the textlines from the large connected components, the
skeleton operation was performed on connected components.
2.2 Machine learning based methods
Apart from Ad hoc methods, few studies are available using various machine learning based
methods also. In a recent study [14] towards TLS in historical documents in Latin script,
a fully convolutional network (FCN) based architecture has been proposed to detect the
baselines. The horizontal projection profile method has been applied to the detected baseline
to calculate the height of the character. Finally, the textline has been cropped by after adding
some margin to the height of the character. However, FCN architecture based methods also
learn the noises like ink stain, humidity spots, etc. present in the document due to ageing
of the document. Moysset et al. [20] used the recurrent neural network (RNN) to segment
the paragraph and textlines of historical documents. Only the count of textlines in each
paragraph was provided in the dataset used by the study. The RNN took the document image
vertically to detect horizontal lines of characters. The skewness in the textline is the layout
challenge of the TLS as well as the HTR. Vo et al. [31] obtained the line map using the FCN
model and the line adjacency graphs were used to classify the touching text components into
textlines. However, this method cannot identify a textline properly if skewness is present as
it does not generate any region of interest (ROI) using region proposal technique.
In the HTR system, each pixel of the document image belongs to either foreground or
background. Gr¨uning et al. [6] used this concept and applied the FCN based architecture
to classify the pixel label. The clustering approach was then used to build baselines. In
another study [1], convolutional recurrent neural network (CRNN) based architecture with
visual geometry group (VGG)-16 [16] as a backbone network was proposed to segment the
textlines in ancient historical documents in Latin script. The training follows incremental

approach with only 10% of manually annotated data. The feature matrix were generated
by convolution operation followed by the long short-term memory (LSTM) to detect the
baselines. Renton et al. [27] applied FCN with dilated convolution to calculate the x-height
of the core text to identify the textline. The FCN resulted in a spatial feature heatmaps in low
resolution of the input document image. The pixel-level labelling was applied to the original
resolution input image. To reflect the features of the input image with original resolution
in the spatial feature heatmaps, the dilating convolutions, unpooling, and deconvolution
operations could be the solutions.
The survey shows that the existing studies are not capable to resolve all the layout, skew-
ness, interline overlapping, and preservation challenges of ancient handwritten historical
documents. But, the proposed faster R-CNN architecture based method can overcome all
the above said challenges as it relies on generation of various ROIs using region proposal
network (RPN).
3 Dataset description
In this work, a new dataset on ancient handwritten historical Devanagari documents has
been generated as no dataset is publicly available in this problem area. The ancient his-
torical document images are in very delicate condition. So, developing a dataset of such
type of documents is not an easy task. The documents have been collected from various
libraries, museums, and archaeological departments. The documents collected in this dataset
are different manuscripts and scriptures in Devanagari script of varying fields from 15th to
18th century originated from central India. The Devanagari script has been widely used to
write different Indo-Aryan languages like Hindi, Marathi, Sanskrit, Sindhi, Konkani, Bun-
delkhandi, Nepali, Bodo, and Maithili. The documents have been collected in image form
using a digital camera. Ayurveda, Religious, Astrological, Vastu, Vedic literature, Tantra,
Ved-vedant, and Political history are the fields of different manuscripts and scriptures col-
lected to generate this dataset. The text skewness and interline overlapping are present in
almost every document images of all the categories of the generated dataset, whereas the
page torn, faded text, and dark spots due to ink or humidity are present mainly in the sam-
ples of Ayurveda, Ved-vedant, Tantra, and Religious manuscripts. Figures 5, 6, 7 and 8 show
four different ancient handwritten document images in Devanagari script in the fields of
Ayurveda, Ved-vedant, and tantra respectively. The document samples presented Figs. 6, 7
and 8 containing the dark spot, faded text, and torn page respectively.

4 Proposed method
The present investigation proposes an end-to-end method for TLS in ancient historical
handwritten documents by proposing a modified form of the conventional faster R-CNN
structure. Ren et al. [26] proposed the conventional faster R-CNN for object detection. The
faster R-CNN can detect an object of varying sizes with various simple as well as complex
backgrounds and in various adverse situations. This robust behaviour of faster R-CNN has
been used to overcome the various challenges of TLS such as text skewness, dark spots, and
torn pages present in the ancient document images in the present work.
The conventional faster R-CNN uses the VGG-16 as a backbone network to extract the
spatial features, whereas the proposed modified form of faster R-CNN structure has used
Resnet50 [9] as a backbone network as the present system has delivered the better perfor-
mance (the results may be seen in Table 8) using this backbone network in comparison to
using VGG-16 as a backbone network in faster R-CNN structure. Apart from this, the con-
ventional faster R-CNN uses nine different anchor boxes whereas the proposed modified
form of faster R-CNN has used fifteen different anchor boxes. The output of the Resnet50
is a feature matrix of size 25 x 25 x 2048. The proposed architecture of faster R-CNN pro-
duces various region proposals using a single RPN utilizing the feature matrix produce by
Resnet50. An additional ROI pooling layer has been used to select some of the region pro-
posals out of all generated by the RPN. The proposed modified architecture of the faster
R-CNN is presented in Fig. 10.

4.1 Backbone network
The Resnet50 has been used as a backbone network in the proposed modified model of
faster R-CNN. A 3D feature matrix has been obtained from the last layer of Resnet50 cor-
responding to the input document image. In the present investigation, Resnet50 consists of
49 convolution layers divided into four stages, whereas the conventional Resnet50 architec-
ture contains 50 different layers. The fully connected layer of conventional Resnet50 has
not been used in the present investigation. Each stage contains varying numbers of convo-
lution blocks, where each block contains three convolution layers. The detailed statistics of
the number of convolution blocks in each stage as well as the number of filters used in each
layer are presented in Table 2.
In Resnet50, the feature matrix generated after a nth convolution layer is merged with
the matrix generated from (n+3)th layer, where n varies from 1 to 46 with a step size of 3.
This characteristic of Resnet50 provides residual behaviour to the backbone network. The
Resnet50 provides faster convergence to the proposed modified model of faster R-CNN in
comparison to the conventional faster R-CNN. It also shows the superior performance and
lesser training time of the proposed modified form of the faster R-CNN in comparison to
the conventional faster R-CNN model (Section 5 may be seen).

4.2 Region proposal network
The RPN uses a small network where a single window slides over the convolutional fea-
ture matrix obtained from the last layer of Resnet50 to generate the region proposals. Each
sliding window has been mapped to 256-D Zeiler and Fergus network [33] to obtain spatial
features for every location. The RPN predicts multiple region proposals for each location
of the feature matrix simultaneously. Each region proposal is known as an anchor box. As
many anchors are there in one sliding window, that many region proposals are generated in
each location of the feature matrix. The centre point of the anchor box coincides with the
centre point of each sliding window. Different aspect ratios and scales are associated with
these anchors. In the present work, three different scales and five different aspect ratios,
yielding a total of fifteen anchor boxes for each location have been used. On the other hand,
the conventional faster R-CNN uses a total of nine different anchor boxes with different
aspect ratios. The total number of anchor boxes for the convolutional feature matrix of size
H x W is H x W x k, where (H,W) denotes the total number of rows and columns of the
convolutional feature matrix respectively and k is the total number of anchors generated
for each location of the feature matrix. The statistics of different anchor boxes used in the
present investigation are presented in Table 3.
The aforementioned multi-scale anchors at every location of the output feature matrix has
allowed us to avoid the extensive selective search [30] complexity to compute the proposals
from the feature matrix. The positive labels have been assigned to the anchors satisfying the
following condition:

4.3 Region of interest pooling
In the present work, the RPN has produced several region proposals of varying sizes. Each
proposal is represented using a five tuple (i,x,y,w,h), where i indicates the index of the
proposal, and (x,y,w,h) are the coordinates of the rectangular detecting box (proposal). The
ROI pooling layer has projected these proposals onto the feature matrix obtained from the
last layer of Resnet50. The inputs to the ROI pooling layer are the feature matrix and the
positive labelled proposals generated by the RPN. In the present work, ‘1000’ ROI proposals
out of total positive labelled proposals generated by the RPN have been selected by the ROI
pooling layer. The value “1000” has been decided using Bayesian optimization technique.
The ROI pooling layer has also performed the max-pooling operation on the feature matrix
corresponding to each proposal generated by the RPN. Each proposal selected after ROI
pooling layer has been fixed to 7 x 7 x 256 dimension and termed as ROI. There is one
advantage of the proposed architecture is that all the ROI proposals generated from the RPN
use same feature matrix obtained from the last layer of Resnet50.
4.4 Detection of textline
The proposed architecture in the present investigation has performed two different tasks—
classification of the textline and a bounding box around the classified textline. The ROI
obtained from the ROI pooling layer has been fed to the detection network (as shown in
Fig. 10) one at a time. The detection network consists of two fully connected layers fol-
lowed by the output layer. The output layer can act as a classifier to detect the textline as
well as regressor to generate the rectangular box coordinates around the detected textline.
The softmax function has been used as an activation function in the output layer to predict
whether the textline is present or not. If the extracted probability score at the output layer
of the detection network is greater than or equal to ‘0.5’, then the output layer predicts the
existence of the textline, otherwise the textline does not exist. If textline exists, then the
regressor will give output of four coordinates of the rectangular box around this detected
textline.
4.5 Training scheme
The proposed architecture has been trained using end-to-end method. During training, the
document sample images from the training set have been provided as the inputs to the pro-
posed architecture. The GT of each training sample has been developed manually. All the
positive anchors generated from a single training image sample have been fed to the ROI
pooling layer during training of the architecture. The loss is calculated as the sum of the dif-
ference between actual (i.e. GT) and predicted (i.e. output provided by detection network).
If there is a textline present in the GT and the output layer classifier predicted it as “Not
textline”, then this will be counted as a loss.

In the proposed architecture, the total loss is the sum of the classification loss [26] and

computed at the output layer of the detection network separately. The total loss calculated
at the output layer of the detection network has been back propagated to the architecture for
fine tuning. The classification loss and regression loss calculated during the training of the
proposed architecture are presented in Fig. 11. The validation loss measures the total loss

6 Conclusion and future work
The segmentation of the textlines in the ancient historical handwritten documents in
Devanagari script is difficult due to the interline spacing, skewness, dark spots, faded text,
etc. This investigation proposes a novel method of TLS in ancient handwritten document
images in Devanagari script. The proposed system overcomes various drawbacks found in
existing TLS systems based on projection profile and grouping methodology. The proposed
system is capable to detect the textline correctly even if the documents are faded, or the
dark spots present on the textlines or the interline overlapping and skewness exist in the
documents. The proposed system is more cost effective because it uses the RPN algorithm
to generate the region proposals instead of selective search method. The results demon-
strate that the proposed system outperforms the state-of-the-art TLS methods as well as the
conventional faster R-CNN based method of TLS in ancient historical handwritten Devana-
gari document images. The major drawback of the proposed system is when it detects any
skewed textlines for few samples the detecting box of the textline overlaps some text por-
tions of the previous and next textlines. Another limitation of the proposed system is it
cannot detect the textlines correctly for few samples if interline overlapping exist.
This work can be extended to segment the textlines in various ancient historical docu-
ments in other Indian as well as non-Indian scripts. The attempt will also be made in future
to perform the word and character level segmentation in the ancient handwritten document
images. Among other possible directions, it has been planned to extend this work up to the
recognition of each handwritten word in the entire ancient document in Devanagari script.
Another possible future direction of this work is to recognize the handwritten text in the
ancient historical documents in other non-Indian scripts as well.
